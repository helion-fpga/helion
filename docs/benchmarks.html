Results

Ten real designs with identical RTL: Helion 2.0.5 against Yosys 0.69 with nextpnr-ecp5, measured on 2026-10-10 on Linux x86_64. Helion targeted its HL10T die (8,192 LUT6); the baseline targeted a Lattice LFE5U-25F (24k LUT4) at 100 MHz. Every number below is measured; the caveats follow the table.

How to read these numbers

  • Different fabrics. Helion's LUT6 die and a Lattice LUT4 part are not silicon-to-silicon comparable; absolute LUT counts and MHz should not be compared head to head.
  • Runtime. Helion ran 3 to 100 times faster in wall-clock time on identical RTL (AES-128: 1.9 s against about 5.5 minutes). Helion's flow is a straight pipeline; Yosys spends its time in ABC and nextpnr in annealing placement.
  • Area. Yosys with ABC still produced 2 to 5 times fewer LUTs. Part of that is LUT4 against LUT6 accounting; most of it is ABC's logic optimization. Closing that gap is ongoing work.
  • Timing. Every completed Helion run met the 10 ns target, but Helion's delay model is simpler and more optimistic than nextpnr's. Calibrating it is on the roadmap.
  • Frontend coverage. Helion's SystemVerilog frontend parsed all ten designs, including three the baseline could not.
